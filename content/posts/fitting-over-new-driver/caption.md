A new driver won't fix a lie angle that's wrong by two degrees.

Most amateurs play irons that were never matched to them. If the lie is too upright, the toe sits off the ground and the face points left at impact. Too flat and it points right. You then build a swing to correct a fault the club created.

A fitting takes an hour and usually changes two things: lie angle and shaft. Both cost a fraction of a new club and both move the ball more than a new head will.

Get measured first. Then decide if you still want the driver.
