The shot you duffed on the last hole is finished. The water on the eighteenth isn't real yet. Both of them feel urgent while you're standing over a ball that has nothing to do with either.

Golf is unusually cruel this way: there's a long walk between shots, and the mind fills it. The players who score well aren't calmer people — they've just built a habit of starting fresh at the ball.

One ball. One target. One swing. Then walk, and do it again.
