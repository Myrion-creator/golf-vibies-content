Squeeze the handle and you slow the clubhead down.

Tension in your hands travels straight up your forearms into your shoulders. Tight shoulders can't rotate, and a body that can't rotate makes the arms do the work. That's where the lost distance goes — not into the hit.

On a scale of one to ten, where ten is white-knuckled, you want about a four. Firm enough that the club doesn't move in your hands, loose enough that you could waggle it.

The check: take your grip, then have someone try to pull the club out. If it slides, you're too soft. If your forearms are hard, you're too tight.
