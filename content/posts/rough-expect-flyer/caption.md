Grass between the clubface and the ball doesn't always cost you distance. Sometimes it adds it.

When the lie is light, blades slide between face and ball and kill the backspin. Less spin means less climb and more roll — the flyer. The ball comes out hot and runs well past your number. That's how people end up over the back of a green from the rough.

Read the lie first:
→ Ball sitting up, light grass: expect a flyer, take less club
→ Ball buried: expect a loss, take more club, steeper swing, accept the advance

The rough punishes autopilot more than it punishes bad swings.
