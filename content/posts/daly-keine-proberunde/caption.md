Ninth alternate. Never saw the course. Won by three.

1991 PGA Championship. Wednesday, 5:30pm: John Daly is told he has moved up the alternate list — with no guarantee of a spot. He gets in his car in Memphis anyway and drives through the night to Indianapolis, reaching his hotel at 1:30am.

While he's driving, Nick Price withdraws to attend the birth of his son. That's the opening. Daly has never set foot on Crooked Stick and has no time for a practice round, so he does the next best thing: he hires Price's caddie, Jeff "Squeeky" Medlen, who knows every yard of it.

He wins his first major by three shots.

The same instinct cost him, too. Bay Hill, 1998: six balls in the water on the par-5 sixth and an 18 on a single hole. Twenty minutes earlier he had been two under for the tournament. He walked off the green eleven over.

His own verdict is the most John Daly sentence ever spoken: "I had the courage to keep going for it, but I didn't have the wisdom to bail out right."
