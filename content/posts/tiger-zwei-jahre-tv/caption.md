He was on national television before he could spell his own name.

6 October 1978, The Mike Douglas Show: a two-year-old hits a driver and putts against Bob Hope. Jimmy Stewart is on the same episode. Douglas had booked him after spotting him in a news clip about toddlers at a driving range.

Twenty-one years later came the Nike juggling ad. Nobody planned it — he was on a lunch break from a different shoot, bouncing a ball off a wedge to kill time, and the director happened to look over.

It took four takes. The routine had to run exactly 28 seconds, and every time the director called "ten seconds left", he shanked it. Before take four he asked them to say nothing until five seconds were left. That one aired.

And his name isn't Tiger. He was born Eldrick Tont Woods and has never legally changed it. Where Tiger comes from is a story for another post.
